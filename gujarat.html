🪔 Gujarat

Vibrant Traditions & Delightful Flavours

Welcome to Gujarat, a vibrant land known for its colourful festivals, rich traditions, historic heritage, and diverse vegetarian cuisine. Gujarati food is famous for its balance of sweet, salty, spicy, and tangy flavours. From the vegetable-filled Undhiyu to the delicate Khandvi, comforting Gujarati Kadhi, and creamy Basundi, every dish reflects the warmth and cultural richness of Gujarat.

🪔 About Gujarat Cuisine

Gujarati cuisine is one of India's most distinctive regional food traditions. It is predominantly vegetarian and is known for combining several flavours in a single meal. Sweet, salty, spicy, and sour notes are often carefully balanced to create dishes that are both comforting and flavourful.

Undhiyu is one of Gujarat's most famous traditional dishes. It is a mixed vegetable preparation made with seasonal vegetables, herbs, spices, and a flavourful stuffing. Traditionally associated with winter and festivals, Undhiyu represents the rich agricultural heritage of Gujarat.

Khandvi is another popular Gujarati delicacy made from gram flour and yogurt. The smooth batter is cooked, spread into thin layers, rolled into delicate pieces, and topped with a tempering of mustard seeds, sesame seeds, and curry leaves.

Gujarati Kadhi is a light and comforting yogurt-based preparation with a mildly sweet and tangy flavour. Basundi, a rich dessert made by slowly reducing milk and adding sugar, cardamom, and nuts, is another beloved sweet enjoyed during festivals and celebrations.

🥘 Undhiyu

Undhiyu is one of Gujarat's most famous traditional dishes. It is a flavourful mixed-vegetable preparation traditionally made with seasonal vegetables, herbs, spices, and a delicious coconut-based stuffing. The dish is especially popular during winter and festive occasions. Its combination of fresh vegetables and aromatic spices makes it a celebrated part of Gujarati cuisine.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 1 cup Small Brinjals
  • 1 cup Surti Papdi
  • 1 cup Green Peas
  • 1 cup Small Potatoes
  • 1 cup Raw Banana, Cubed
  • ½ cup Purple Yam, Cubed
  • ½ cup Fresh Methi Leaves
  • ½ cup Grated Coconut
  • 2 tbsp Sesame Seeds
  • 2 tbsp Coriander Leaves, Chopped
  • 1 tbsp Ginger-Green Chili Paste
  • 1 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tsp Coriander-Cumin Powder
  • ½ tsp Red Chili Powder
  • 2 tbsp Jaggery
  • 2 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • 3 tbsp Oil
  • Salt to Taste
  • Water as Required

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Wash and prepare all the vegetables thoroughly.
  2. Make small slits in the brinjals and potatoes without cutting them completely.
  3. Prepare the stuffing by mixing grated coconut, sesame seeds, chopped coriander, ginger-green chili paste, turmeric, coriander-cumin powder, chili powder, salt, and a little oil.
  4. Add jaggery and lemon juice to the stuffing mixture.
  5. Fill the prepared brinjals and potatoes with the stuffing.
  6. Heat oil in a heavy-bottomed pan.
  7. Add the remaining stuffing and gently sauté it.
  8. Add the Surti papdi, green peas, raw banana, purple yam, and methi leaves.
  9. Arrange the stuffed brinjals and potatoes over the vegetables.
  10. Add a little water and cover the pan.
  11. Cook on low heat until all the vegetables become tender.
  12. Gently mix the vegetables without breaking the stuffed pieces.
  13. Cook for a few more minutes until the flavours combine well.
  14. Check the seasoning and adjust salt if required.
  15. Serve hot with puri, roti, or Gujarati flatbread.

⏱ Cooking Time

60 Minutes

🍽 Serves

5 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use fresh seasonal vegetables for the best flavour. Cook Undhiyu slowly over low heat so that the vegetables remain intact and absorb the aromatic stuffing.

🌟 Fun Fact

The name Undhiyu is associated with the traditional cooking method in which the dish was historically cooked in an earthen pot placed upside down. It is particularly popular in Gujarat during the winter season.

🌯 Khandvi

Khandvi is a delicate and popular Gujarati snack made from gram flour and yogurt. The smooth batter is cooked until it becomes soft and spreadable, then quickly spread into thin layers and rolled into beautiful bite-sized pieces. It is usually finished with a tempering of mustard seeds, sesame seeds, curry leaves, and green chilies. Khandvi is loved for its soft texture and mildly tangy flavour.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 1 cup Gram Flour (Besan)
  • 1 cup Plain Yogurt
  • 2 cups Water
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • ½ tsp Ginger-Green Chili Paste
  • Salt to Taste
  • 1 tbsp Grated Coconut
  • 2 tbsp Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1 tsp Sesame Seeds
  • 8–10 Curry Leaves
  • 2 Green Chilies, Chopped
  • 1 tbsp Oil

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Take gram flour in a mixing bowl and add yogurt.
  2. Gradually add water while whisking continuously to avoid lumps.
  3. Add turmeric powder, ginger-green chili paste, and salt.
  4. Mix everything until a smooth batter is formed.
  5. Pour the batter into a heavy-bottomed pan.
  6. Cook over low to medium heat while stirring continuously.
  7. Continue stirring until the mixture becomes thick and smooth.
  8. To check the consistency, spread a small amount of the mixture on a greased plate. It should spread easily and become firm after cooling slightly.
  9. Quickly spread the cooked mixture into a very thin layer on a large greased plate or smooth surface.
  10. Allow it to cool for a few minutes.
  11. Cut the thin layer into long strips.
  12. Carefully roll each strip into a tight spiral.
  13. Arrange the rolls neatly on a serving plate.
  14. Heat oil in a small pan for tempering.
  15. Add mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.
  16. Add sesame seeds, curry leaves, and chopped green chilies.
  17. Pour the tempering over the Khandvi rolls.
  18. Garnish with grated coconut and fresh coriander leaves.
  19. Serve fresh as a snack or light breakfast.

⏱ Cooking Time

30 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

The batter must be cooked to the right consistency. Spread it immediately while it is hot, because it becomes difficult to spread once it starts cooling. A thin layer gives Khandvi its soft and delicate texture.

🌟 Fun Fact

Khandvi is known for its delicate yellow rolls and smooth texture. Preparing perfectly thin Khandvi requires careful cooking and quick spreading of the batter.

🥣 Gujarati Kadhi

Gujarati Kadhi is a traditional yogurt-based dish known for its light, creamy texture and distinctive sweet-and-tangy flavour. It is prepared with yogurt, gram flour, spices, and a simple tempering. Gujarati Kadhi is commonly served with steamed rice, khichdi, or rotla and is a comforting part of everyday Gujarati meals.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups Plain Yogurt
  • 2 tbsp Gram Flour (Besan)
  • 3 cups Water
  • 1–2 tbsp Sugar or Jaggery
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • ½ tsp Ginger-Green Chili Paste
  • Salt to Taste
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • ½ tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1 Dry Red Chili
  • 8–10 Curry Leaves
  • 1 tbsp Ghee
  • 2 tbsp Fresh Coriander Leaves

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Take yogurt in a large bowl and whisk it until smooth.
  2. Add gram flour and mix thoroughly to remove any lumps.
  3. Gradually add water while whisking continuously.
  4. Add turmeric powder, ginger-green chili paste, sugar or jaggery, and salt.
  5. Pour the mixture into a deep saucepan.
  6. Cook over medium heat while stirring continuously.
  7. Bring the kadhi to a gentle boil.
  8. Reduce the heat and simmer for about 10–15 minutes.
  9. Keep stirring occasionally so that the yogurt mixture remains smooth.
  10. Heat ghee in a small tempering pan.
  11. Add mustard seeds and cumin seeds.
  12. Allow the seeds to splutter.
  13. Add dry red chili and curry leaves.
  14. Pour the tempering over the prepared kadhi.
  15. Garnish with fresh coriander leaves.
  16. Serve hot with steamed rice, khichdi, or Gujarati rotla.

⏱ Cooking Time

25 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Whisk the yogurt and gram flour thoroughly before heating to prevent lumps. Keep stirring while bringing the mixture to a boil, and adjust the sweetness according to your preference.

🌟 Fun Fact

Gujarati Kadhi is known for its characteristic balance of sweet and tangy flavours. Unlike some other regional kadhi varieties, the Gujarati version is generally lighter and often has a noticeable sweetness.

🍮 Basundi

Basundi is a rich and creamy traditional Indian dessert that is especially popular in Gujarat and Maharashtra. It is prepared by slowly reducing full-fat milk until it becomes thick and creamy, then sweetened with sugar and flavoured with cardamom, saffron, and nuts. Basundi is commonly prepared during festivals, celebrations, and special occasions.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 1 Litre Full-Cream Milk
  • ½ cup Sugar
  • 4 Green Cardamoms
  • A Few Saffron Strands
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Almonds
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Pistachios
  • 1 tbsp Chopped Cashews
  • 1 tbsp Rose Water
  • 1 tbsp Milk Powder (Optional)

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Pour full-cream milk into a heavy-bottomed pan.
  2. Place the pan over medium heat and bring the milk to a gentle boil.
  3. Reduce the heat and simmer the milk slowly.
  4. Stir frequently and scrape the cream that forms on the sides back into the milk.
  5. Continue cooking until the milk reduces and becomes noticeably thick.
  6. Add sugar and mix well.
  7. Add crushed cardamom and saffron strands.
  8. Continue cooking on low heat for a few more minutes.
  9. Add chopped almonds, pistachios, and cashews.
  10. Add rose water for a gentle aroma if desired.
  11. Mix everything well and turn off the heat.
  12. Allow the Basundi to cool slightly.
  13. It can be served warm or chilled according to preference.
  14. Garnish with additional chopped nuts and saffron before serving.

⏱ Cooking Time

50 Minutes

🍽 Serves

5 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use full-cream milk and cook it slowly over low heat for a rich texture. Stir regularly to prevent the milk from sticking to the bottom of the pan and scrape the cream from the sides back into the dessert.

🌟 Fun Fact

Basundi is a traditional milk-based dessert enjoyed during festivals and celebrations. Its rich texture comes from slowly reducing the milk rather than using a thickener as the main ingredient.
