🏛️ Bihar

Ancient Heritage & Traditional Flavours

Welcome to Bihar, a land of ancient history, spiritual heritage, vibrant festivals, and rich culinary traditions. Bihari cuisine is known for its wholesome ingredients, rustic cooking methods, aromatic spices, and comforting flavours. From the smoky Litti Chokha to the delicate Khaja, festive Thekua, and sweet Malpua, every dish reflects the diverse cultural heritage and traditional food practices of Bihar.

🏛️ About Bihar Cuisine

Bihar has a rich culinary heritage shaped by its agricultural traditions, festivals, local ingredients, and cultural history. Traditional Bihari food is generally simple, wholesome, and flavourful, with wheat, rice, lentils, vegetables, dairy products, and seasonal ingredients forming an important part of everyday meals.

Litti Chokha is one of Bihar's most iconic dishes. Litti consists of wheat flour dough filled with a roasted mixture of sattu, herbs, spices, and mustard oil. It is traditionally served with Chokha, a preparation made from roasted vegetables such as potato, brinjal, and tomato.

Bihar is also famous for its traditional sweets and festive delicacies. Khaja is a layered, crispy sweet that is particularly associated with the town of Silao. Thekua is a popular traditional snack and sweet prepared especially during Chhath Puja using wheat flour, jaggery, ghee, and dry fruits.

Malpua is another beloved sweet enjoyed during festivals and special occasions. These soft and sweet pancakes are prepared from a flour-based batter and are often served with thickened milk or other traditional accompaniments. Together, these dishes showcase the rich and diverse flavours of Bihar.

🥔 Litti Chokha

Litti Chokha is one of the most famous traditional dishes of Bihar. Litti is made from wheat flour and filled with a flavourful mixture of roasted gram flour, known as sattu, along with spices, herbs, and mustard oil. It is traditionally served with Chokha, a rustic preparation of roasted potato, brinjal, and tomato. The smoky aroma and earthy flavours make Litti Chokha a beloved Bihari specialty.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups Wheat Flour
  • 1 cup Sattu (Roasted Gram Flour)
  • 1 Large Brinjal
  • 2 Medium Potatoes
  • 2 Tomatoes
  • 2 Green Chilies, Finely Chopped
  • 1 tbsp Ginger, Grated
  • 2 tbsp Mustard Oil
  • 1 tsp Ajwain
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • ½ tsp Red Chili Powder
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • Salt to Taste
  • Water as Required

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Take wheat flour in a large bowl and add a little salt and ajwain.
  2. Add water gradually and knead into a soft dough.
  3. Cover the dough and allow it to rest for about 15 minutes.
  4. For the filling, mix sattu with chopped green chilies, ginger, cumin seeds, lemon juice, mustard oil, and salt.
  5. Add a little water if required and prepare a moist filling.
  6. Divide the wheat dough into small portions.
  7. Flatten each portion and place some sattu filling in the centre.
  8. Bring the edges together and seal the filling completely.
  9. Shape the filled dough into round balls.
  10. Cook the litti in a preheated oven, tandoor, or traditional roasting method until the outside becomes golden and cooked.
  11. For Chokha, roast the brinjal and tomatoes until their skin becomes charred and the vegetables are soft.
  12. Boil the potatoes until tender and peel them.
  13. Mash the roasted brinjal, tomatoes, and potatoes together.
  14. Add chopped green chilies, coriander, mustard oil, salt, and lemon juice.
  15. Mix everything well to prepare the Chokha.
  16. Serve the hot litti with freshly prepared Chokha.

⏱ Cooking Time

60 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Keep the sattu filling slightly moist so that the litti remains flavourful inside. Roasting the vegetables well gives the Chokha its characteristic smoky taste. A little mustard oil adds an authentic Bihari flavour.

🌟 Fun Fact

Litti Chokha is strongly associated with Bihar's traditional food culture. Litti can be roasted over charcoal or fire, giving it a distinctive smoky aroma and rustic character.

🍯 Khaja

Khaja is a traditional layered sweet associated with Bihar and is especially famous in the town of Silao. It is made from thin layers of flour dough that are folded, shaped, and deep-fried until crisp and flaky. The fried sweet is then coated or soaked in sugar syrup, giving it a delicate sweetness and crunchy texture. Khaja is commonly enjoyed during festivals, celebrations, and special occasions.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups All-Purpose Flour
  • 3 tbsp Ghee
  • ½ cup Water
  • 1 cup Sugar
  • ½ cup Water for Sugar Syrup
  • 2 tbsp Cornflour
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • Oil for Deep Frying
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Pistachios
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Almonds

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Take all-purpose flour in a mixing bowl.
  2. Add two tablespoons of ghee and mix well.
  3. Gradually add water and knead into a smooth and firm dough.
  4. Cover the dough and allow it to rest for about 20 minutes.
  5. Divide the dough into equal portions.
  6. Roll each portion into a thin circular sheet.
  7. Brush the surface with ghee and sprinkle a little cornflour.
  8. Place another rolled sheet on top and repeat the process to create layers.
  9. Roll the layered sheets together and cut them into small pieces.
  10. Gently flatten each piece without destroying the layers.
  11. Heat oil in a deep pan over medium heat.
  12. Deep-fry the shaped dough pieces until they become crisp and golden.
  13. Remove them from the oil and drain the excess oil.
  14. For the syrup, heat sugar and water in another pan.
  15. Cook until a slightly sticky sugar syrup is formed.
  16. Add cardamom powder to the syrup.
  17. Dip the fried khaja into the warm sugar syrup for a short time.
  18. Remove and allow the excess syrup to drain.
  19. Garnish with chopped almonds and pistachios.
  20. Allow the khaja to cool before serving.

⏱ Cooking Time

50 Minutes

🍽 Serves

6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Roll the dough into thin layers and handle them gently to create a flaky texture. Fry the khaja over medium heat so that the layers become crisp without browning too quickly.

🌟 Fun Fact

Silao in Bihar is particularly famous for its traditional khaja. The sweet's distinctive flaky layers and crisp texture make it one of Bihar's well-known delicacies.

🌾 Thekua

Thekua is a traditional Bihari sweet made mainly with wheat flour, jaggery, ghee, and dry fruits. It is especially associated with Chhath Puja and is prepared as an important festive offering. Thekua has a crisp outer texture with a mildly sweet and aromatic centre, making it a cherished traditional delicacy of Bihar.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups Wheat Flour
  • 1 cup Jaggery
  • ½ cup Water
  • 4 tbsp Ghee
  • 2 tbsp Grated Coconut
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Cashews
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Almonds
  • 1 tbsp Sesame Seeds
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • ½ tsp Fennel Seeds
  • Oil or Ghee for Frying

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Break the jaggery into small pieces and place it in a saucepan.
  2. Add water and heat gently until the jaggery dissolves completely.
  3. Strain the jaggery syrup to remove any impurities and keep it aside.
  4. Take wheat flour in a large mixing bowl.
  5. Add grated coconut, chopped cashews, almonds, sesame seeds, fennel seeds, and cardamom powder.
  6. Add ghee and mix everything well.
  7. Gradually add the warm jaggery syrup and combine the mixture.
  8. Knead gently to form a firm dough. Add only enough syrup to bring the dough together.
  9. Divide the dough into small portions.
  10. Shape each portion into a small thick disc.
  11. Press the surface gently using a traditional Thekua mould or fork to create a decorative pattern.
  12. Heat oil or ghee in a deep pan over medium-low heat.
  13. Carefully place the shaped Thekua into the oil.
  14. Fry slowly until the Thekua becomes deep golden brown and crisp.
  15. Remove from the oil and place on a plate lined with paper.
  16. Allow the Thekua to cool completely before serving.

⏱ Cooking Time

45 Minutes

🍽 Serves

8 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Do not make the dough too soft. A firm dough helps the Thekua retain its shape and creates a crisp texture. Fry it slowly over medium-low heat for an even golden colour.

🌟 Fun Fact

Thekua has a special place in Bihari culture and is strongly associated with Chhath Puja. It is traditionally prepared as a sacred offering and is also enjoyed as a festive sweet.

🥞 Malpua

Malpua is a traditional Indian sweet pancake enjoyed across Bihar, especially during festivals and special occasions. It is prepared from a soft flour-based batter flavoured with cardamom and fennel, then gently fried until golden and soaked in sugar syrup. Malpua is often served warm with thickened milk or rabri, making it a rich and delicious festive dessert.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 1 cup All-Purpose Flour
  • 2 tbsp Semolina
  • ½ cup Milk
  • ½ cup Water
  • ½ cup Sugar
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • ½ tsp Fennel Seeds
  • 1 tbsp Grated Khoya (Optional)
  • Ghee or Oil for Frying
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Pistachios
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Almonds

🍯 For Sugar Syrup

  • 1 cup Sugar
  • ½ cup Water
  • 2 Cardamom Pods
  • A Few Saffron Strands

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Take all-purpose flour and semolina in a mixing bowl.
  2. Add cardamom powder and fennel seeds.
  3. Gradually add milk and water while whisking continuously.
  4. Mix until a smooth, lump-free batter is formed.
  5. Add grated khoya if desired and mix well.
  6. Allow the batter to rest for about 20–30 minutes.
  7. Meanwhile, prepare the sugar syrup by heating sugar and water in a saucepan.
  8. Add cardamom pods and saffron strands to the syrup.
  9. Cook until a slightly sticky sugar syrup is formed.
  10. Heat ghee or oil in a shallow pan over medium heat.
  11. Pour a small ladleful of batter into the pan.
  12. Spread it gently into a small round shape.
  13. Cook until the edges become golden and crisp.
  14. Flip carefully and cook the other side.
  15. Remove the golden malpua from the pan.
  16. Place it in the warm sugar syrup for a short time.
  17. Remove the malpua and allow the excess syrup to drain.
  18. Garnish with chopped almonds and pistachios.
  19. Serve warm with rabri or thickened milk if desired.

⏱ Cooking Time

40 Minutes

🍽 Serves

6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Allowing the batter to rest helps develop a softer texture. Keep the frying temperature moderate so the malpua cooks evenly and develops a beautiful golden colour.

🌟 Fun Fact

Malpua is enjoyed in Bihar during festivals and special occasions. It is often paired with rabri or thickened milk to create a rich and festive dessert.
