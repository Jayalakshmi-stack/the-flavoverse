🌴 About Kerala Cuisine

Kerala, popularly known as "God's Own Country", is one of India's most beautiful states, famous for its backwaters, coconut groves, spice plantations, and rich cultural heritage. Kerala cuisine is loved for its fresh ingredients, unique cooking methods, and delicious flavors. Coconut, curry leaves, black pepper, cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, and fresh seafood are widely used in everyday cooking.

Rice is the staple food of Kerala and is served with a variety of curries, vegetable dishes, seafood, and pickles. Traditional meals are often enjoyed on fresh banana leaves, especially during festivals and celebrations. The world-famous Onam Sadya is a grand vegetarian feast consisting of more than twenty delicious dishes served together, representing Kerala's hospitality and culture.

Kerala also offers a wide range of breakfast dishes such as Appam, Puttu, Idiyappam, and Dosa, which are served with coconut-based curries and stews. Sweet dishes like Ada Pradhaman and Payasam are prepared during festivals and family gatherings, making every celebration special and memorable.

The food of Kerala is a perfect combination of health, taste, and tradition. Whether it is spicy Malabar Biryani, creamy Fish Molee, comforting Kerala Stew, or delicious desserts, every recipe reflects the state's rich history and culinary excellence. Kerala cuisine continues to attract food lovers from all over the world with its authentic flavors and timeless recipes.

🍛 Malabar Biryani

Malabar Biryani is one of Kerala's most famous traditional dishes, originating from the Malabar region. It is prepared using fragrant Jeerakasala rice, tender chicken or mutton, aromatic spices, fried onions, raisins, and cashew nuts. Unlike other biryanis, Malabar Biryani has a unique mild flavor and rich aroma, making it a favorite during weddings, festivals, and family celebrations.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 500 g Chicken or Mutton
  • 2 cups Jeerakasala (Kaima) Rice
  • 2 Onions (sliced)
  • 2 Tomatoes (chopped)
  • 1 cup Yogurt
  • Ginger-Garlic Paste
  • Green Chilies
  • Mint Leaves
  • Coriander Leaves
  • Turmeric Powder
  • Red Chilli Powder
  • Garam Masala
  • Whole Spices (Cinnamon, Cloves, Cardamom)
  • Ghee
  • Salt
  • Cashew Nuts & Raisins

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Wash the Jeerakasala rice thoroughly and soak it for about 20 minutes before cooking.
  2. Heat ghee in a pan and fry sliced onions until golden brown. Roast the cashew nuts and raisins separately and keep them aside.
  3. In the same pan, sauté ginger-garlic paste, green chilies, onions, and tomatoes until soft.
  4. Add turmeric powder, red chilli powder, garam masala, and salt. Mix everything well.
  5. Add the chicken or mutton along with yogurt and cook until the meat becomes tender and fully cooked.
  6. Cook the rice separately until it is about 90% done. Drain any excess water.
  7. Layer the cooked rice over the prepared meat. Sprinkle fried onions, roasted cashews, raisins, mint leaves, coriander leaves, and a little ghee on top.
  8. Cover the pan with a tight lid and cook on low heat (dum) for 20–25 minutes so the flavors blend perfectly.
  9. Gently mix the layers before serving and enjoy hot with raita, pickle, and boiled egg.

⏱ Cooking Time

1 Hour 30 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4–6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Always use Jeerakasala rice instead of Basmati rice for authentic Malabar Biryani. Cooking on low flame (dum) gives the biryani its signature aroma and rich taste.

🌟 Fun Fact

Malabar Biryani is one of Kerala's most celebrated dishes and is commonly served at weddings, festivals, and special family occasions throughout the Malabar region.

🐟 Fish Molee

Fish Molee is one of Kerala's most famous seafood dishes, known for its mild flavor and creamy coconut milk gravy. Fresh fish is gently cooked with onions, tomatoes, ginger, garlic, green chilies, and aromatic spices. Unlike spicy fish curries, Fish Molee has a rich, delicate taste and is traditionally served with appam, bread, idiyappam, or steamed rice. It is a popular choice for family meals and festive occasions.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 500 g Fresh Fish (King Fish, Pomfret or Seer Fish)
  • 2 Onions (thinly sliced)
  • 2 Tomatoes (chopped)
  • 1 cup Thick Coconut Milk
  • ½ cup Thin Coconut Milk
  • 2 Green Chilies (slit)
  • 1 tbsp Ginger (finely chopped)
  • 1 tbsp Garlic (finely chopped)
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • ½ tsp Black Pepper Powder
  • Curry Leaves
  • 2 tbsp Coconut Oil
  • Salt to Taste

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Clean and wash the fish pieces thoroughly.
  2. Heat coconut oil in a pan and sauté onions until soft.
  3. Add ginger, garlic, green chilies, curry leaves, and tomatoes. Cook until the tomatoes become soft.
  4. Add turmeric powder, pepper powder, and salt. Mix well.
  5. Pour in the thin coconut milk and bring it to a gentle boil.
  6. Carefully add the fish pieces and cook for about 10 minutes until the fish becomes tender.
  7. Add the thick coconut milk and cook on a low flame for another 3–5 minutes without boiling.
  8. Turn off the heat and allow the curry to rest for a few minutes before serving.
  9. Serve hot with appam, idiyappam, bread, or steamed rice.

⏱ Cooking Time

35 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Always add the thick coconut milk at the end and cook on a low flame. This keeps the curry smooth, creamy, and prevents the coconut milk from curdling.

🌟 Fun Fact

Fish Molee was influenced by Kerala's coastal culture and is one of the state's most loved seafood recipes. Its mild coconut flavor makes it popular with people of all ages.

🍮 Ada Pradhaman

Ada Pradhaman is one of Kerala's most famous traditional desserts and is considered the king of all Payasams. It is prepared using rice ada, jaggery, thick coconut milk, and roasted cashew nuts and raisins. This rich and creamy dessert is an important part of the famous Onam Sadya and is enjoyed during festivals, weddings, and family celebrations. Its delicious sweetness and aromatic flavor make it one of Kerala's most loved desserts.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 1 cup Rice Ada
  • 2 cups Thick Coconut Milk
  • 1 cup Thin Coconut Milk
  • 250 g Jaggery
  • 3 tbsp Ghee
  • 10 Cashew Nuts
  • 10 Raisins
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • 2 tbsp Coconut Pieces (optional)

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Boil the rice ada in water until it becomes soft. Drain the water and rinse with cold water.
  2. Heat jaggery with a little water until it completely melts. Strain the syrup to remove any impurities.
  3. Add the cooked rice ada to the jaggery syrup and cook for 5–8 minutes until well combined.
  4. Pour in the thin coconut milk and cook on a low flame for about 10 minutes.
  5. Add the thick coconut milk and gently stir without allowing it to boil.
  6. Mix in the cardamom powder for a pleasant aroma.
  7. In a small pan, heat ghee and roast cashew nuts, raisins, and coconut pieces until golden brown.
  8. Add the roasted ingredients to the Payasam and mix gently.
  9. Serve warm or chilled according to your preference.

⏱ Cooking Time

45 Minutes

🍽 Serves

6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use freshly prepared coconut milk and good-quality jaggery for the best taste. Always add the thick coconut milk at the end and avoid boiling it to keep the dessert rich and creamy.

🌟 Fun Fact

Ada Pradhaman is considered one of the most important desserts served during the traditional Onam Sadya. Many families prepare it as a symbol of celebration, happiness, and togetherness.

🥣 Kerala Stew

Kerala Stew is a light, creamy, and mildly spiced curry that is loved throughout Kerala. It is traditionally prepared with vegetables or chicken cooked in fresh coconut milk, flavored with ginger, green chilies, curry leaves, and whole spices. The stew has a delicate aroma and smooth texture, making it a perfect companion for appam, idiyappam, bread, or chapati. It is a popular breakfast and dinner dish enjoyed by families across the state.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 Potatoes (cubed)
  • 1 Carrot (sliced)
  • 1 Onion (sliced)
  • 1 cup Green Peas
  • 2 Green Chilies (slit)
  • 1 tbsp Ginger (chopped)
  • 2 cups Thick Coconut Milk
  • ½ cup Thin Coconut Milk
  • 1 Cinnamon Stick
  • 3 Cloves
  • 2 Cardamom Pods
  • Curry Leaves
  • 2 tbsp Coconut Oil
  • Salt to Taste
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Heat coconut oil in a pan and add cinnamon, cloves, and cardamom until fragrant.
  2. Add sliced onions, ginger, green chilies, and curry leaves. Sauté until the onions become soft.
  3. Add potatoes, carrots, and green peas. Mix well with a little salt.
  4. Pour in the thin coconut milk and cook until the vegetables become soft and tender.
  5. Add the thick coconut milk and stir gently over a low flame.
  6. Do not boil after adding the thick coconut milk, as it may curdle.
  7. Adjust the salt and simmer for 2–3 minutes.
  8. Garnish with fresh coriander leaves and curry leaves.
  9. Serve hot with appam, idiyappam, bread, or chapati.

⏱ Cooking Time

35 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use fresh coconut milk for the richest flavor. Always cook the stew on a low flame after adding the thick coconut milk to maintain its smooth and creamy texture.

🌟 Fun Fact

Kerala Stew is believed to have influences from Kerala's historic spice trade. Today, it remains one of the state's most comforting dishes and is especially popular with soft appam for breakfast and festive meals.

🌴 Kerala Food Facts

🥥 Coconut Paradise

Kerala is known as the Land of Coconuts. Fresh coconut, coconut milk, and coconut oil are used in many traditional dishes, giving them their rich and authentic taste.

🌶️ Spice Capital

Kerala is famous for producing black pepper, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, and nutmeg. These spices make Kerala cuisine aromatic and flavorful.

🍛 Onam Sadya

Onam Sadya is Kerala's grand festive meal, consisting of more than twenty delicious vegetarian dishes served together during the Onam festival.

🐟 Seafood Special

Kerala's long coastline provides fresh seafood throughout the year. Fish, prawns, crab, and mussels are commonly used in many traditional recipes.

🍮 Traditional Desserts

Payasam varieties like Ada Pradhaman and Palada Payasam are an important part of Kerala festivals, weddings, and family celebrations.
