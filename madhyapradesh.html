🏰 Madhya Pradesh

Heart of India & Hearty Flavours

Welcome to Madhya Pradesh, the "Heart of India", known for its historic forts, ancient temples, forests, and a food culture that is as warm as its people. Cuisine here ranges from the crunchy, savoury street snacks of Indore to the rustic wheat-and-ghee meals of the Malwa region. From the beloved Indori Poha to the creamy Bhutte Ka Kees, the hearty Dal Bafla, and the rich, melt-in-the-mouth Mawa Bati, every dish reflects the culinary heritage of Madhya Pradesh.

Explore Recipes

🏰 About Madhya Pradesh Cuisine

Madhya Pradesh cuisine is shaped by its central location, fertile farmland, and the traditions of its many regions. Wheat, corn, lentils, milk, ghee, and everyday spices form the base of traditional cooking. The food is known for being wholesome, comforting, and often lightly sweet and savoury at the same time.

Indori Poha is one of the state's most popular breakfast dishes. Flattened rice is gently cooked with onions, peanuts, curry leaves, and turmeric, and is traditionally finished with a squeeze of lemon, a sprinkle of sev, and a dash of jeeravan masala.

Bhutte Ka Kees is a creamy snack made from grated corn cooked in milk with mild spices, especially enjoyed during the monsoon season. Dal Bafla is a classic rustic meal of baked wheat dough balls dipped in ghee and served with spiced dal.

Mawa Bati is one of the region's well-loved sweets. Soft, khoya-based dumplings stuffed with dry fruits are fried and soaked in fragrant sugar syrup, making them a favourite during festivals and celebrations.

🥣 Indori Poha

📖 About the Dish

Indori Poha is the most loved breakfast of Indore and Madhya Pradesh. Flattened rice is cooked with onions, peanuts, curry leaves, turmeric, and a hint of sugar and lemon for a perfectly balanced sweet, tangy, and savoury taste. It is traditionally topped with fine sev, chopped onions, fresh coriander, and a sprinkle of jeeravan masala, and is often enjoyed with a hot cup of tea.

🌍 Origin

  • State : Madhya Pradesh
  • Cuisine : Traditional Malwa Cuisine

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups Thick Poha (Flattened Rice)
  • 1 Potato, Finely Diced
  • 1 Onion, Finely Chopped
  • 2 tbsp Raw Peanuts
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 8–10 Curry Leaves
  • 2 Green Chilies, Chopped
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tsp Sugar
  • 1 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • 2 tbsp Oil
  • Salt to Taste
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • Sev, for Topping
  • Jeeravan Masala, for Sprinkling

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Place the poha in a strainer and rinse it quickly under running water.
  2. Drain well and let it rest for about 5 minutes until it turns soft.
  3. Mix in salt, sugar, and half of the turmeric powder gently.
  4. Heat oil in a pan or kadai.
  5. Add the peanuts and fry until they turn crisp, then keep them aside.
  6. In the same oil, add mustard seeds and let them splutter.
  7. Add curry leaves and green chilies.
  8. Add the diced potato and cook with a lid on until tender.
  9. Add the chopped onions and sauté until soft and translucent.
  10. Add the remaining turmeric powder and mix well.
  11. Add the prepared poha and mix gently without mashing it.
  12. Cover and steam on low heat for 2–3 minutes.
  13. Add the fried peanuts and lemon juice and mix lightly.
  14. Turn off the heat and garnish with fresh coriander.
  15. Serve hot topped with sev, chopped onions, and a sprinkle of jeeravan masala.

⏱ Cooking Time

25 Minutes

🍽 Serves

3 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use thick poha and do not soak it for too long, otherwise it turns mushy. A little sugar and a final squeeze of lemon give Indori Poha its signature sweet-and-tangy taste.

🌟 Fun Fact

Poha with jalebi is a popular breakfast combination in Indore, and the dish is a way of life in the city's morning food culture.

🌽 Bhutte Ka Kees

📖 About the Dish

Bhutte Ka Kees is a famous Indori snack made from freshly grated corn cooked in milk with ghee, mild spices, and green chilies. The corn is slowly simmered until the mixture turns soft, creamy, and lightly spicy. It is garnished with coriander, grated coconut, and a squeeze of lemon, and is especially popular during the rainy season.

🌍 Origin

  • State : Madhya Pradesh
  • Cuisine : Traditional Malwa Cuisine

🥘 Ingredients

  • 4 Fresh Corn Cobs, Grated
  • 2 cups Milk
  • 2 tbsp Ghee
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • ½ tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 2 Green Chilies, Finely Chopped
  • 1 tsp Grated Ginger
  • ¼ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tsp Sugar
  • 1 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • 2 tbsp Fresh Grated Coconut
  • Salt to Taste
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Remove the kernels from the corn cobs and grate them into a coarse pulp.
  2. Heat ghee in a heavy-bottomed pan.
  3. Add mustard seeds and cumin seeds and let them splutter.
  4. Add the green chilies and grated ginger and sauté briefly.
  5. Add the grated corn and mix well.
  6. Sauté the corn on low heat for 5–6 minutes to remove the raw smell.
  7. Add turmeric powder and mix thoroughly.
  8. Pour in the milk gradually while stirring.
  9. Cover and cook on low heat, stirring occasionally.
  10. Continue cooking until the corn turns soft and the milk reduces to a creamy consistency.
  11. Add salt and sugar and mix well.
  12. Turn off the heat and add the lemon juice.
  13. Garnish with grated coconut and fresh coriander leaves.
  14. Serve hot.

⏱ Cooking Time

30 Minutes

🍽 Serves

3 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use fresh, tender corn and keep stirring on low heat so the milk does not catch at the bottom. Add lemon juice only after turning off the heat to keep the milk from splitting.

🌟 Fun Fact

Bhutte Ka Kees is a signature street snack of Indore, and is closely associated with the city's famous evening food markets.

🍞 Dal Bafla

📖 About the Dish

Dal Bafla is a rustic and satisfying meal from the Malwa region of Madhya Pradesh. Bafla are firm wheat dough balls that are boiled or baked until cooked through, then broken open and soaked in generous amounts of ghee. They are served with a warm, spiced dal, and often accompanied by chutney, onions, and a sweet such as ladoo.

🌍 Origin

  • State : Madhya Pradesh
  • Cuisine : Traditional Malwa Cuisine

🥘 Ingredients

For the Bafla

  • 2 cups Whole Wheat Flour
  • 2 tbsp Semolina (Sooji)
  • ½ tsp Ajwain (Carom Seeds)
  • 4 tbsp Ghee, for the dough
  • Salt to Taste
  • Water as Required
  • Melted Ghee, for dipping

For the Dal

  • 1 cup Toor Dal
  • 1 Tomato, Chopped
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 2 tbsp Ghee
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 4 Garlic Cloves, Chopped
  • 2 Dry Red Chilies
  • A Pinch of Asafoetida (Hing)
  • 1 tsp Red Chili Powder
  • Salt to Taste
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Mix wheat flour, semolina, ajwain, salt, and ghee in a large bowl until crumbly.
  2. Add water gradually and knead into a stiff dough.
  3. Rest the dough for 15 minutes.
  4. Divide the dough into equal portions and shape into firm round balls.
  5. Preheat the oven to 200°C.
  6. Bake the baflas for 25–30 minutes, turning them occasionally, until golden brown and cooked through.
  7. Wash the toor dal and pressure cook it with water, turmeric, and salt until soft.
  8. Mash the cooked dal lightly.
  9. Add chopped tomato and simmer for a few minutes.
  10. Heat ghee in a small pan and add cumin seeds.
  11. Add garlic, dry red chilies, and asafoetida and sauté until fragrant.
  12. Add red chili powder and immediately pour the tempering over the dal.
  13. Simmer for 2–3 minutes and garnish with coriander.
  14. Lightly crack each hot bafla and dip it into melted ghee.
  15. Serve the baflas hot with the dal.

⏱ Cooking Time

75 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Knead the dough stiff so the baflas stay firm. Dipping them in warm ghee while they are still hot lets them soak up flavour and turn soft inside.

🌟 Fun Fact

Dal Bafla is closely related to Rajasthan's Dal Baati, and is a classic celebration and festival meal in the Malwa region.

🍮 Mawa Bati

📖 About the Dish

Mawa Bati is a rich traditional sweet of Madhya Pradesh made from khoya (mawa). Soft khoya dough is stuffed with chopped dry fruits, shaped into small round balls, fried gently in ghee until golden, and soaked in warm, cardamom-scented sugar syrup. It is served warm or at room temperature and is a favourite at festivals and special occasions.

🌍 Origin

  • State : Madhya Pradesh
  • Cuisine : Traditional Madhya Pradesh Sweets

🥘 Ingredients

  • 200 g Khoya (Mawa), Grated
  • 3 tbsp All-Purpose Flour
  • ¼ tsp Baking Soda
  • 1 tbsp Milk, if needed
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Almonds and Pistachios
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • 1 cup Sugar
  • 1 cup Water
  • A Few Strands of Saffron
  • Ghee for Frying

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Mix the sugar and water in a pan and boil until the syrup turns slightly sticky.
  2. Add the saffron and half of the cardamom powder, then keep the syrup warm.
  3. Mix the chopped dry fruits with the remaining cardamom powder for the stuffing.
  4. Combine the grated khoya, all-purpose flour, and baking soda in a bowl.
  5. Gently knead into a smooth, soft dough, adding a little milk only if required.
  6. Divide the dough into small equal portions.
  7. Flatten each portion slightly and place a little dry fruit stuffing in the centre.
  8. Seal and roll into smooth, crack-free balls.
  9. Heat ghee in a pan on low heat.
  10. Fry the balls slowly in batches until they turn evenly golden brown.
  11. Remove the fried balls and drain excess ghee.
  12. Soak them in the warm sugar syrup for 30 minutes.
  13. Garnish with a few chopped pistachios.
  14. Serve warm or at room temperature.

⏱ Cooking Time

50 Minutes

🍽 Serves

6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Fry the balls on low heat so they cook through without breaking. Make sure the syrup is warm, not boiling, so the batis soak it up evenly and stay soft.

🌟 Fun Fact

Mawa Bati is closely associated with the festive sweet traditions of central India, and is often prepared during weddings and celebrations.
