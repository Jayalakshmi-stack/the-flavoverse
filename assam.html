🌿 Assam

Tea Gardens & Traditional Flavours

Welcome to Assam, a beautiful northeastern state known for its lush tea gardens, mighty Brahmaputra River, vibrant tribal traditions, and rich culinary heritage. Assamese cuisine is known for its simple cooking methods, fresh local ingredients, aromatic herbs, rice-based dishes, and balanced flavours. From the tangy Masor Tenga to the comforting Duck Meat Curry, traditional Pitha, and nutritious Khar, every dish reflects the unique culture and natural richness of Assam.

🛕 About Assam Cuisine

Assam is known for its traditional cuisine, which has a strong connection with the state's temples, festivals, and cultural heritage. Odia food is generally simple, wholesome, and prepared with locally available ingredients. The cuisine beautifully balances mild spices, vegetables, lentils, rice, dairy products, and traditional sweets.

Rice is an important part of everyday meals in Assam. Dishes such as Masor Tenga combine lentils and vegetables to create a nutritious and comforting meal. Duck Meat Curry, a fragrant sweet rice preparation, is another traditional dish enjoyed during festivals and special occasions.

Assam is also famous for its wonderful chhena-based sweets. Khar, meaning "burnt cheese," is a traditional dessert prepared from fresh chhena and sugar and baked until it develops a caramelized crust. Pitha is another delicious sweet made from fried chhena patties soaked in thickened sweet milk.

Temple cuisine has a special place in Odia food culture. The Mahaprasad of the Jagannath Temple in Puri is an important part of Assam's culinary heritage. These traditional food practices have been preserved through generations and continue to represent the cultural identity of Assam.

🥬 Khar

Khar is a traditional Assamese preparation known for its distinctive alkaline flavour and simple cooking style. It is traditionally prepared using raw papaya or other vegetables along with an alkaline extract made from the ash of dried banana peels or banana plants. Khar is an important part of Assamese traditional cuisine and is often enjoyed with steamed rice.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups Raw Papaya, Peeled and Cubed
  • 3 tbsp Khar Water or Alkaline Extract
  • 1 tbsp Mustard Oil
  • 2 Green Chilies, Slit
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tbsp Ginger, Finely Chopped
  • 2 tbsp Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • Salt to Taste
  • 2 cups Water

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Wash, peel, and cut the raw papaya into small cubes.
  2. Heat mustard oil in a cooking pan.
  3. Add mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.
  4. Add chopped ginger and slit green chilies.
  5. Sauté gently until the ginger becomes aromatic.
  6. Add the cubed raw papaya and turmeric powder.
  7. Mix everything well and cook for a few minutes.
  8. Add water and cover the pan.
  9. Cook until the papaya becomes soft and tender.
  10. Add the traditional khar water or alkaline extract gradually.
  11. Mix gently and simmer for a few minutes.
  12. Adjust salt according to taste.
  13. Garnish with fresh coriander leaves.
  14. Serve warm with steamed rice.

⏱ Cooking Time

30 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use the alkaline extract carefully because its flavour can be strong. Add it gradually and adjust according to taste. Fresh raw papaya gives the dish a pleasant texture and absorbs the traditional flavours well.

🌟 Fun Fact

Khar is one of the distinctive dishes of Assamese cuisine. The traditional alkaline ingredient gives the dish its characteristic flavour and makes it different from many other Indian vegetable preparations.

🐟 Masor Tenga

Masor Tenga is one of the most popular traditional fish dishes of Assam. It is a light and refreshing fish curry known for its distinctive tangy flavour. Fresh fish is gently cooked with tomatoes and a souring ingredient, creating a simple and comforting dish that is traditionally enjoyed with steamed rice.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 500 g Fresh Fish Pieces
  • 2 Medium Tomatoes, Chopped
  • 2 Green Chilies, Slit
  • 1 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • 1 tbsp Mustard Oil
  • 1 tbsp Lemon Juice
  • 1 tbsp Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • ½ tsp Ginger, Grated
  • 2 cups Water
  • Salt to Taste

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Clean the fish pieces thoroughly and drain the excess water.
  2. Rub the fish pieces with turmeric powder and a little salt.
  3. Heat mustard oil in a pan.
  4. Lightly fry the fish pieces on both sides and keep them aside.
  5. In the same pan, add mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.
  6. Add grated ginger and slit green chilies.
  7. Add the chopped tomatoes and cook until they become soft.
  8. Add turmeric powder and a little salt.
  9. Pour in the water and bring the curry to a gentle boil.
  10. Carefully place the fried fish pieces into the curry.
  11. Cover and cook on low heat for about 8–10 minutes.
  12. Add lemon juice towards the end of cooking.
  13. Simmer for another 2 minutes and turn off the heat.
  14. Garnish with fresh coriander leaves.
  15. Serve hot with steamed rice.

⏱ Cooking Time

35 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Do not overcook the fish after adding it to the curry. Add the souring ingredient towards the end so that the curry keeps its fresh and pleasant tangy flavour.

🌟 Fun Fact

The word “Tenga” refers to the characteristic sour or tangy flavour of this Assamese preparation. Masor Tenga is especially appreciated for its light and refreshing taste.

🦆 Duck Meat Curry

Duck Meat Curry is a traditional Assamese dish enjoyed especially during festivals, family gatherings, and special occasions. The curry is prepared with tender duck meat, potatoes, aromatic spices, ginger, garlic, and local ingredients. Its rich flavour and comforting aroma make it a cherished part of Assamese festive cuisine.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 500 g Duck Meat, Cut into Pieces
  • 2 Medium Potatoes, Cubed
  • 2 Onions, Finely Chopped
  • 1 Tomato, Chopped
  • 1 tbsp Ginger-Garlic Paste
  • 2 Green Chilies, Slit
  • 1 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tsp Cumin Powder
  • 1 tsp Coriander Powder
  • ½ tsp Black Pepper Powder
  • 2 Bay Leaves
  • 1 Cinnamon Stick
  • 2 Cardamoms
  • 2 Cloves
  • 2 tbsp Mustard Oil
  • 2 cups Water
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • Salt to Taste

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Clean the duck meat thoroughly and cut it into medium-sized pieces.
  2. Rub the duck pieces with turmeric powder and salt.
  3. Heat mustard oil in a heavy-bottomed pan.
  4. Add bay leaves, cinnamon, cardamom, and cloves.
  5. Add the chopped onions and sauté until they become lightly golden.
  6. Add ginger-garlic paste and green chilies.
  7. Cook until the raw aroma of the ginger and garlic disappears.
  8. Add chopped tomatoes and cook until they become soft.
  9. Add cumin powder, coriander powder, turmeric powder, and black pepper powder.
  10. Mix the spices well and cook for a few minutes.
  11. Add the marinated duck pieces and sauté them with the masala.
  12. Cook until the duck releases its natural juices and becomes lightly browned.
  13. Add the cubed potatoes and mix gently.
  14. Add water and cover the pan.
  15. Cook on low heat until the duck meat and potatoes become tender.
  16. Check the seasoning and adjust salt if necessary.
  17. Garnish with fresh coriander leaves.
  18. Serve hot with steamed rice.

⏱ Cooking Time

60 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Cooking the duck slowly allows the meat to become tender and helps the spices develop a deeper flavour. Mustard oil adds a traditional Assamese character to the curry.

🌟 Fun Fact

Duck meat dishes are traditionally enjoyed in Assam during special occasions and family celebrations. Duck with potatoes is one of the well-known combinations in Assamese home cooking.

🥮 Pitha

Pitha is one of the most beloved traditional foods of Assam and holds a special place in Assamese festivals, especially during Bihu. These rice-based delicacies are prepared in different varieties using rice flour, coconut, sesame, jaggery, and other locally available ingredients. Pitha can be steamed, roasted, or cooked on a pan and is enjoyed as a traditional festive treat.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups Rice Flour
  • 1 cup Grated Coconut
  • ¾ cup Jaggery, Grated
  • 2 tbsp Sesame Seeds
  • 1 cup Warm Water
  • 2 tbsp Rice Flour for Filling
  • 1 tbsp Ghee
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • A Small Pinch of Salt

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Heat a pan and add the grated coconut.
  2. Add grated jaggery and cook gently until the jaggery melts.
  3. Add sesame seeds and cardamom powder.
  4. Mix well and cook until the filling becomes slightly dry.
  5. Turn off the heat and allow the filling to cool.
  6. Place rice flour in a mixing bowl and add a small pinch of salt.
  7. Gradually add warm water and mix to form a soft dough.
  8. Knead the dough gently until it becomes smooth.
  9. Divide the dough into small portions.
  10. Flatten each portion and place a small amount of coconut-jaggery filling in the centre.
  11. Fold the edges carefully and seal the filling inside.
  12. Shape the pitha according to your preferred traditional style.
  13. Steam the pitha until it becomes firm and cooked.
  14. Alternatively, it can be gently roasted on a pan with a little ghee.
  15. Allow the pitha to cool slightly before serving.
  16. Serve warm as a traditional Assamese festive delicacy.

⏱ Cooking Time

40 Minutes

🍽 Serves

6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Keep the rice-flour dough soft and smooth so that the pitha does not crack while shaping. Freshly grated coconut and good-quality jaggery give the filling a delicious traditional flavour.

🌟 Fun Fact

Pitha is strongly associated with Assamese festivals, especially Bihu. Different regions and communities of Assam prepare several varieties of pitha using traditional methods passed down through generations.

🌿 Assam Food Facts

Khar is one of Assam's most famous desserts. Fresh chhena is baked with sugar until the top develops a delicious caramelized crust.
