🏰 Maharashtra

Royal Heritage & Authentic Flavours

Welcome to Maharashtra, a land of historic forts, vibrant festivals, diverse traditions, and delicious regional cuisine. Maharashtrian food is known for its bold flavours, aromatic spices, wholesome ingredients, and wonderful variety. From the sweet and comforting Puran Poli to the spicy Misal Pav, famous Pav Bhaji, and festive Modak, every dish reflects the rich cultural heritage of Maharashtra.

🏰 About Maharashtra Cuisine

Maharashtrian cuisine is a diverse food tradition influenced by the state's geography, agriculture, festivals, and cultural heritage. The cuisine includes a wide variety of vegetarian and non-vegetarian dishes, with ingredients such as lentils, vegetables, grains, coconut, peanuts, jaggery, and aromatic spices commonly used.

Puran Poli is one of Maharashtra's most famous traditional sweets. It is a soft flatbread filled with a sweet mixture of cooked lentils, jaggery, cardamom, and nutmeg. It is especially popular during festivals and celebrations.

Misal Pav is a popular Maharashtrian street food made with a spicy sprouted-legume curry topped with farsan, onions, coriander, and other garnishes. It is traditionally served with soft pav and is known for its bold and spicy flavours.

Pav Bhaji is another much-loved dish that became particularly popular in Mumbai. It consists of a spicy mashed vegetable preparation served with buttered pav. Modak, a sweet dumpling traditionally filled with coconut and jaggery, holds a special place in Maharashtrian festive cuisine, especially during Ganesh Chaturthi.

🫓 Puran Poli

📖 About the Dish

Puran Poli is one of Maharashtra's most beloved traditional sweet dishes. It is a soft flatbread filled with a sweet mixture called puran, prepared from cooked chana dal, jaggery, cardamom, and nutmeg. It is especially popular during festivals and celebrations and is traditionally served with ghee or warm milk.

🌍 Origin

  • State : Maharashtra
  • Cuisine : Traditional Maharashtrian Cuisine

🥘 Ingredients

  • 1 cup Chana Dal
  • 1 cup Jaggery
  • 2 cups Wheat Flour
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • ¼ tsp Nutmeg Powder
  • 2 tbsp Ghee
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • Water as Required
  • Salt – a Small Pinch
  • Ghee for Cooking

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Wash the chana dal thoroughly and soak it for about 30 minutes.
  2. Cook the dal with enough water until it becomes soft.
  3. Drain the excess water completely and keep the cooked dal aside.
  4. Add the cooked dal and jaggery to a heavy-bottomed pan.
  5. Cook the mixture on medium heat until the jaggery melts and the mixture becomes thick.
  6. Add cardamom powder and nutmeg powder.
  7. Mix well and cook until the mixture becomes firm enough to shape.
  8. Allow the puran mixture to cool.
  9. Grind the mixture until it becomes smooth and lump-free.
  10. In another bowl, combine wheat flour, turmeric, salt, and a little ghee.
  11. Add water gradually and knead into a soft dough.
  12. Cover the dough and allow it to rest for about 20–30 minutes.
  13. Divide the dough and puran into equal portions.
  14. Take one portion of dough and flatten it gently.
  15. Place a portion of puran in the centre and seal the edges.
  16. Roll it gently into a round flatbread.
  17. Heat a tawa over medium heat.
  18. Place the Puran Poli on the hot tawa.
  19. Cook both sides until golden spots appear.
  20. Apply a little ghee and serve warm.

⏱ Cooking Time

60 Minutes

🍽 Serves

6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Cook the puran until it becomes thick but not dry. Kneading the dough well makes the Puran Poli soft and easier to roll without breaking.

🌟 Fun Fact

Puran Poli is closely associated with Maharashtrian festivals and celebrations. It is traditionally enjoyed with ghee and is especially popular during festive meals.

🌶️ Misal Pav

📖 About the Dish

Misal Pav is one of Maharashtra's most popular and flavourful street foods. It is made with a spicy sprouted-legume curry called misal, which is topped with crunchy farsan, chopped onions, coriander, and lemon juice. It is traditionally served with soft pav and is loved for its combination of spicy, tangy, and crunchy textures.

🌍 Origin

  • State : Maharashtra
  • Cuisine : Traditional Maharashtrian Cuisine

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups Mixed Sprouts
  • 1 Onion, Chopped
  • 2 Tomatoes, Chopped
  • 1 Potato, Cubed
  • 1 tbsp Ginger-Garlic Paste
  • 2 tbsp Oil
  • 1 tsp Mustard Seeds
  • ½ tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tsp Red Chili Powder
  • 1 tbsp Misal Masala
  • ½ tsp Garam Masala
  • Salt to Taste
  • 3 cups Water
  • 1 cup Farsan or Mixture
  • 1 Onion, Finely Chopped for Garnishing
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • Lemon Wedges
  • Pav – As Required

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Wash the mixed sprouts thoroughly and keep them ready.
  2. Heat oil in a deep pan.
  3. Add mustard seeds and allow them to splutter.
  4. Add cumin seeds and sauté for a few seconds.
  5. Add chopped onions and cook until they become soft.
  6. Add ginger-garlic paste and sauté until the raw aroma disappears.
  7. Add chopped tomatoes and cook until they become soft.
  8. Add turmeric powder, red chili powder, misal masala, garam masala, and salt.
  9. Mix the spices well with the onion and tomato mixture.
  10. Add the mixed sprouts and cubed potato.
  11. Pour in the required amount of water and mix gently.
  12. Cover and cook until the sprouts and potatoes become tender.
  13. Adjust the consistency by adding a little more water if required.
  14. Simmer the misal for a few minutes so that the flavours combine well.
  15. Transfer the hot misal into serving bowls.
  16. Top each serving with crunchy farsan.
  17. Add finely chopped onions and fresh coriander leaves.
  18. Serve with lemon wedges and warm pav.

⏱ Cooking Time

40 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Adjust the amount of chili and misal masala according to your taste. Add the farsan just before serving so that it remains crunchy.

🌟 Fun Fact

Misal Pav is a beloved Maharashtrian street food and is enjoyed for breakfast, snacks, or a filling meal. Different regions of Maharashtra have their own versions of Misal with varying levels of spice.

🍞 Pav Bhaji

📖 About the Dish

Pav Bhaji is one of Maharashtra's most popular street foods, especially associated with Mumbai. It is a delicious combination of mashed vegetables cooked with aromatic spices and served with soft, buttered pav. The dish is colourful, flavourful, and commonly enjoyed as a quick meal or evening snack.

🌍 Origin

  • State : Maharashtra
  • Cuisine : Traditional Maharashtrian Cuisine

🥘 Ingredients

  • 3 Potatoes, Boiled
  • 1 cup Cauliflower, Chopped
  • 1 cup Green Peas
  • 1 Carrot, Chopped
  • 1 Capsicum, Chopped
  • 2 Tomatoes, Chopped
  • 1 Onion, Chopped
  • 1 tbsp Ginger-Garlic Paste
  • 2 tbsp Pav Bhaji Masala
  • 1 tsp Red Chili Powder
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 2 tbsp Butter
  • 1 tbsp Oil
  • Salt to Taste
  • ½ cup Water
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • Lemon Wedges
  • Pav – As Required

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Boil the potatoes, cauliflower, green peas, and carrot until they become soft.
  2. Mash the cooked vegetables and keep them aside.
  3. Heat butter and oil in a large pan or tawa.
  4. Add chopped onions and sauté until they become soft.
  5. Add ginger-garlic paste and cook until the raw aroma disappears.
  6. Add chopped tomatoes and cook until they become soft and mushy.
  7. Add capsicum and sauté for a few minutes.
  8. Add turmeric powder, red chili powder, pav bhaji masala, and salt.
  9. Mix the spices thoroughly with the cooked vegetables.
  10. Add the mashed potatoes, cauliflower, peas, and carrot.
  11. Add a little water and mix everything well.
  12. Mash the vegetables gently using a masher while cooking.
  13. Cook the bhaji until it becomes thick, smooth, and flavourful.
  14. Add another spoonful of butter for extra richness if desired.
  15. Garnish the bhaji with fresh coriander leaves.
  16. Heat a tawa and add a little butter.
  17. Cut the pav horizontally and toast both sides on the buttered tawa.
  18. Serve the hot bhaji with buttered pav.
  19. Garnish with chopped onions and serve with lemon wedges.

⏱ Cooking Time

40 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Mash the vegetables thoroughly while cooking to get a smooth bhaji. Toast the pav with butter just before serving for the best flavour and texture.

🌟 Fun Fact

Pav Bhaji is strongly associated with Mumbai's street-food culture. It became popular as a quick and filling meal and is now enjoyed throughout India.

🍬 Modak

📖 About the Dish

Modak is a traditional Maharashtrian sweet dumpling made with a soft rice-flour outer covering and a delicious filling of coconut and jaggery. It is especially associated with Ganesh Chaturthi and is traditionally prepared as an offering to Lord Ganesha. The combination of coconut, jaggery, cardamom, and rice flour gives Modak its distinctive taste and aroma.

🌍 Origin

  • State : Maharashtra
  • Cuisine : Traditional Maharashtrian Cuisine

🥘 Ingredients

  • 1 cup Rice Flour
  • 1 cup Water
  • 1 cup Grated Coconut
  • ¾ cup Jaggery
  • 1 tsp Ghee
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Cashews
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Almonds
  • 1 tbsp Poppy Seeds
  • 1 Small Pinch of Salt

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Heat a pan and add grated coconut and jaggery.
  2. Cook the mixture over low heat until the jaggery melts and combines with the coconut.
  3. Add cardamom powder, poppy seeds, cashews, and almonds.
  4. Mix well and cook until the filling becomes slightly thick.
  5. Allow the filling to cool and keep it aside.
  6. For the outer covering, bring one cup of water to a boil.
  7. Add a small pinch of salt and one teaspoon of ghee.
  8. Reduce the heat and gradually add the rice flour.
  9. Mix quickly until the flour absorbs the water.
  10. Cover and allow the mixture to rest for a few minutes.
  11. While the mixture is still warm, knead it gently into a smooth dough.
  12. Divide the dough into small equal portions.
  13. Take one portion and flatten it into a thin circular shape.
  14. Place a spoonful of coconut-jaggery filling in the centre.
  15. Bring the edges together and create small pleats around the filling.
  16. Pinch the pleats together at the top to form the traditional Modak shape.
  17. Prepare the remaining Modaks in the same way.
  18. Place the Modaks in a greased steamer.
  19. Steam them for about 10–15 minutes until the outer covering becomes soft and slightly translucent.
  20. Serve warm with a little ghee.

⏱ Cooking Time

45 Minutes

🍽 Serves

6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Keep the rice-flour dough soft and smooth so that it does not crack while shaping the Modaks. Steam them gently and avoid overcooking to maintain their soft texture.

🌟 Fun Fact

Modak is especially important during Ganesh Chaturthi in Maharashtra. Steamed Ukadiche Modak is one of the most traditional varieties and is commonly prepared as a festive offering.
