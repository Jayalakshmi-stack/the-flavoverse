🛕 Odisha

Temple Heritage & Authentic Flavours

Welcome to Odisha, a land of ancient temples, vibrant traditions, beautiful art, and distinctive cuisine. Odia food is known for its simple preparation, balanced flavours, locally grown ingredients, and deep connection with temple traditions. From the caramelized sweetness of Chhena Poda to the wholesome Dalma, fragrant Kanika, and creamy Rasabali, every dish reflects the rich culinary heritage of Odisha.

🛕 About Odisha Cuisine

Odisha is known for its traditional cuisine, which has a strong connection with the state's temples, festivals, and cultural heritage. Odia food is generally simple, wholesome, and prepared with locally available ingredients. The cuisine beautifully balances mild spices, vegetables, lentils, rice, dairy products, and traditional sweets.

Rice is an important part of everyday meals in Odisha. Dishes such as Dalma combine lentils and vegetables to create a nutritious and comforting meal. Kanika, a fragrant sweet rice preparation, is another traditional dish enjoyed during festivals and special occasions.

Odisha is also famous for its wonderful chhena-based sweets. Chhena Poda, meaning "burnt cheese," is a traditional dessert prepared from fresh chhena and sugar and baked until it develops a caramelized crust. Rasabali is another delicious sweet made from fried chhena patties soaked in thickened sweet milk.

Temple cuisine has a special place in Odia food culture. The Mahaprasad of the Jagannath Temple in Puri is an important part of Odisha's culinary heritage. These traditional food practices have been preserved through generations and continue to represent the cultural identity of Odisha.

🍰 Chhena Poda

Chhena Poda is one of Odisha's most famous traditional desserts. The name means "burnt cheese," and the dessert is prepared using fresh chhena, sugar, cardamom, and dry fruits. It is baked until the outside develops a beautiful caramelized crust while the inside remains soft and creamy. Chhena Poda is especially popular during festivals, celebrations, and special occasions.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 500 g Fresh Chhena
  • ¾ cup Sugar
  • 2 tbsp Semolina
  • 2 tbsp Ghee
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Cashews
  • 2 tbsp Raisins
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Almonds
  • A few Saffron Strands (Optional)

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Place fresh chhena in a large mixing bowl.
  2. Crumble and knead the chhena until it becomes smooth.
  3. Add sugar, semolina, ghee, and cardamom powder.
  4. Mix everything thoroughly until well combined.
  5. Add chopped cashews, almonds, and raisins.
  6. Grease a baking pan with ghee and transfer the mixture into it.
  7. Spread the mixture evenly and smooth the surface.
  8. Bake in a preheated oven at 180°C until the top becomes deep golden brown.
  9. Allow it to cool before removing it from the pan.
  10. Cut into slices and serve warm or chilled.

⏱ Cooking Time

60 Minutes

🍽 Serves

6 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use fresh chhena for the best texture. Baking it slowly allows the top to caramelize beautifully while keeping the inside soft and moist.

🌟 Fun Fact

Chhena Poda is often described as Odisha's signature baked dessert. Its distinctive caramelized crust and soft chhena centre make it different from many other Indian sweets.

🥘 Dalma

Dalma is one of the most popular traditional dishes of Odisha. It is a wholesome preparation made with lentils, raw papaya, pumpkin, potato, raw banana, and other vegetables. The dish is gently seasoned with cumin, ginger, and other aromatic spices. Dalma is commonly served with steamed rice and is loved for its simple, comforting, and nutritious flavours.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 1 cup Toor Dal
  • 1 cup Pumpkin, Cubed
  • 1 Potato, Cubed
  • 1 Raw Banana, Cubed
  • 1 cup Raw Papaya, Cubed
  • 1 Tomato, Chopped
  • 1 tsp Turmeric Powder
  • 1 tsp Cumin Seeds
  • 1 tsp Cumin Powder
  • ½ tsp Ginger, Grated
  • 2 Green Chilies
  • 1 tbsp Ghee
  • 1 Dry Red Chilli
  • 1 Bay Leaf
  • Fresh Coriander Leaves
  • Salt to Taste

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Wash the toor dal thoroughly and drain the water.
  2. Add dal, turmeric, potato, pumpkin, raw banana, raw papaya, tomato, ginger, and salt to a pressure cooker.
  3. Add enough water and cook until the dal and vegetables become soft.
  4. Allow the pressure to release naturally and gently mix the cooked dal and vegetables.
  5. Heat ghee in a small pan for tempering.
  6. Add cumin seeds, dry red chilli, and bay leaf.
  7. Allow the spices to release their aroma without burning them.
  8. Pour the tempering over the cooked dal and vegetables.
  9. Add cumin powder and green chilies.
  10. Mix gently and simmer for a few minutes.
  11. Garnish with fresh coriander leaves.
  12. Serve hot with steamed rice.

⏱ Cooking Time

40 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Use a variety of fresh vegetables for an authentic Dalma. A simple ghee tempering with cumin and dry red chilli gives the dish its characteristic aroma and flavour.

🌟 Fun Fact

Dalma is traditionally associated with Odia home cooking and is also closely connected with Odisha's temple food traditions. It is valued for combining lentils and vegetables in one wholesome dish.

🍚 Kanika

Kanika is a traditional Odia sweet rice preparation made with fragrant rice, sugar, ghee, and aromatic spices. It is known for its beautiful golden colour, mild sweetness, and rich aroma. Kanika is traditionally prepared during festivals, religious occasions, and special celebrations in Odisha.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 2 cups Basmati or Gobindobhog Rice
  • 3 cups Water
  • ½ cup Sugar
  • 3 tbsp Ghee
  • 2 Green Cardamoms
  • 1 Cinnamon Stick
  • 3 Cloves
  • 2 Bay Leaves
  • 2 tbsp Cashews
  • 2 tbsp Raisins
  • 2 tbsp Grated Coconut
  • ½ tsp Turmeric Powder
  • A few Saffron Strands
  • Salt – a Small Pinch

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Wash the rice thoroughly and soak it for about 20 minutes.
  2. Drain the rice and keep it aside.
  3. Heat ghee in a heavy-bottomed pan.
  4. Add bay leaves, cinnamon, cardamom, and cloves.
  5. Add the soaked rice and gently sauté for a few minutes.
  6. Add turmeric powder and a small pinch of salt.
  7. Add water and cook the rice until it is almost tender.
  8. Add sugar and mix gently.
  9. Add saffron strands and cook on low heat until the sugar blends well with the rice.
  10. Fry cashews and raisins in a little ghee until golden.
  11. Add the roasted dry fruits and grated coconut to the rice.
  12. Cover and cook on low heat for another 5 minutes.
  13. Gently fluff the rice and serve warm.

⏱ Cooking Time

40 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Easy

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Cook the rice gently after adding sugar so that the grains remain separate. Good-quality ghee, cardamom, and roasted dry fruits give Kanika its traditional festive aroma.

🌟 Fun Fact

Kanika is a traditional sweet rice dish of Odisha and is closely associated with festive and religious food traditions.

🍮 Rasabali

Rasabali is a traditional Odia dessert made from soft chhena patties that are lightly fried and then soaked in thick, sweetened milk. Flavoured with cardamom and garnished with nuts, Rasabali has a rich, creamy texture and is an important part of Odisha's traditional sweet dishes.

🥘 Ingredients

  • 250 g Fresh Chhena
  • 2 tbsp Semolina
  • 3 tbsp Sugar
  • 1 litre Full Cream Milk
  • 3 tbsp Ghee
  • ½ tsp Cardamom Powder
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Cashews
  • 2 tbsp Chopped Almonds
  • A few Saffron Strands

👩‍🍳 Preparation

  1. Place fresh chhena in a bowl and knead it until smooth.
  2. Add semolina and one tablespoon of sugar and mix well.
  3. Divide the mixture into small portions and shape them into flat, round patties.
  4. Heat ghee in a pan and lightly fry the chhena patties until golden on both sides.
  5. Heat the milk in a heavy-bottomed pan.
  6. Simmer the milk until it reduces and becomes slightly thick.
  7. Add the remaining sugar, cardamom powder, and saffron.
  8. Place the fried chhena patties gently into the warm milk.
  9. Simmer for a few minutes so the patties absorb the sweetened milk.
  10. Garnish with chopped almonds and cashews.
  11. Serve warm or chilled.

⏱ Cooking Time

50 Minutes

🍽 Serves

4 People

⭐ Difficulty

Medium

👨‍🍳 Chef's Tip

Knead the chhena until completely smooth so the patties remain soft. Reduce the milk slowly to create a creamy base and allow the fried patties to absorb the flavour.

🌟 Fun Fact

Rasabali is traditionally associated with Odisha's temple food culture and is especially connected with the Baladevjew Temple in Kendrapara.
